Why do acne scars form?

When acne inflammation goes deeper than the surface layer, the tissue that supports the skin is damaged. If the area does not fill in fully as it heals, or its floor is pulled downward, a depressed scar is left.

  • Deep, long-lasting inflammation. Hard nodules and cysts full of pus leave scars most easily.
  • Squeezing or picking. Forcing out an unripe pimple spreads inflammation sideways and down. Many of the scars we see match where people squeezed at home.
  • Acne in the same place again and again. Damage builds up with no time to recover, which is why repeated acne should be controlled before scar treatment.
  • Chickenpox or injuries. Scratched chickenpox spots or deep wounds can leave round, depressed scars.

How our approach fits with other scar treatments

Fractional lasers, fillers and injections are all used for acne scars, and the right method depends on the scar. Scars with a large loss of volume may be hard to fill with needles or lasers alone, and we will tell you so honestly at the consultation. Raised scars (keloids) need a different treatment, starting with injections at a dermatology clinic, so in that case we will refer you.

  • Depressed scars: Saesal acupuncture (Transtherapy) stimulates each scar so the skin fills itself in.
  • Rough texture and shallow scars over a wide area: pico fractional laser is considered alongside it.
  • Red marks and uneven texture around the scars: Dual Therapy (M-Tone and Hooyun herbal peel).
  • Acne that is still active: treated first, so new scars don't keep forming.

If you have had laser treatment elsewhere, you can still consult us. Please tell us when and what kind of treatment you had.

During scar treatment, please

  • Don't squeeze new pimples. A new scar during treatment sets you back.
  • Use sunscreen every day. Treated skin darkens easily in the sun.
  • Don't rub the treated area. Let scabs fall off on their own.
  • Avoid saunas and alcohol right after treatment. Heat can make redness last longer.
  • Pause scrubs and exfoliants while the skin recovers.
  • Take photos under the same light. Change is slow, so photos show it best.
