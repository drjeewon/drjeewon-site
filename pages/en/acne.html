Adult acne is often less about how much oil you make and more about the conditions that keep repeating. If breakouts appear on the jawline and around the mouth, before your period or in a tiring week, changing your cleanser alone rarely ends them. The basic approach is to calm the pimples you have now with extraction and soothing care, and at the same time work on what keeps bringing them back: your cycle, sleep, digestion and heat in the body.

Why does adult acne happen?

Acne starts when a pore opening is blocked, oil collects (a comedone), and inflammation spreads inside it. What makes adult acne different from teenage acne is that it clearly follows a pattern of when and where.

  • Hormonal cycle. Oil increases and skin becomes more sensitive before a period. The jawline and mouth area react most to this change.
  • Lack of sleep and stress. Skin recovers more slowly and inflammation lasts longer. That is why so many people say it always appears in a busy week.
  • Digestion and eating habits. Late-night meals, alcohol, greasy food and sweet drinks make it worse for many people, as does constipation.
  • Heat rising to the face. In Korean medicine, a face that flushes easily, a dry mouth and red pimples are read as heat moving upward.
  • Picking and squeezing. Squeezing unripe pimples spreads the inflammation and is the most common cause of marks and scars.

If antibiotics or isotretinoin are hard for you

Antibiotics, isotretinoin and topical creams are strong at reducing inflammation quickly. Even so, many people come to us because they cannot take these medicines, or because the acne returns as soon as they stop. Isotretinoin dries the skin and lips, needs blood tests during treatment, and pregnancy must be avoided while taking it and for a month after, so it is difficult for anyone planning a pregnancy. Long courses of antibiotics can upset the stomach, and acne often comes back in the same places afterwards.

For these patients we do two things at once, without acne medication:

  • The acne you have now: the doctor extracts only what is ready that day, then calms the skin with anti-inflammatory pharmacopuncture and soothing care.
  • The conditions that bring it back: your cycle, sleep, digestion and heat pattern are assessed and treated with herbal medicine. If you are planning a pregnancy, please tell us.

Six things you can start today

  • Don't squeeze. Most scars form where pimples were squeezed at home. Many settle if you wait a day.
  • Sleep six to seven hours. Sleep is directly linked to how fast inflammation heals.
  • Cut down on sweet drinks and dairy for a month and see whether it makes a difference. It doesn't help everyone, but it is worth testing.
  • Wash gently twice a day. Washing more irritates the skin and can increase oil.
  • Change pillowcases and masks often. Chin acne reacts to friction and moisture.
  • Write down the dates. Comparing breakouts with your cycle, late nights and drinking shows the cause.

Conditions that look like acne

  • Folliculitis: pus at each hair follicle, often itchy, with no comedones.
  • Seborrheic dermatitis: redness with flaking beside the nose, eyebrows and forehead.
  • Rosacea: cheeks and nose flush red, mixed with small bumps.
  • Perioral dermatitis: small red bumps clustered around the mouth, sometimes after steroid cream.
  • Flat warts: flat bumps that don't come out when squeezed. See flat warts.
