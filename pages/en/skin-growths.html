A skin tag is a hanging flap of skin, milia are white grains of trapped keratin, and seborrheic keratosis is a brown patch where the top layer of skin has thickened. All three are harmless, but their roots differ in shape and depth, so burning them all the same way can leave remnants or marks. We tell them apart first, then remove each according to root thickness, grain depth or lesion thickness.

Why do they appear?

  • Skin tags: friction and constitution. The neck is rubbed all day by hair, collars, necklaces and bag straps. Add sweat and heat and skin tags form easily. They often increase with age, weight gain or pregnancy, and many people have a family tendency.
  • Milia: trapped keratin. When skin can't shed old keratin and traps it in a tiny pocket, a milium forms. Rich eye creams, healing after burns or strong lasers and peels, sun damage and long-term steroid cream can all play a part. Milia on newborns usually disappear within weeks.
  • Seborrheic keratosis: accumulated sun. Years of UV exposure and constitution make surface cells pile up. The backs of the hands are often missed with sunscreen, so we see more of them there now.

Spots that are unusually dark or multi-coloured, irregular with blurred edges, or that have recently grown fast, bled or itched may not be seborrheic keratosis or skin tags. These need a diagnosis with a biopsy at a dermatology clinic before any removal, and we will recommend that at your consultation.

Aftercare and prevention

  • Don't pull off scabs. Apply the healing ointment and let them fall off.
  • Reduce friction on the neck. Avoid necklaces and stiff collars while healing.
  • Keep the backs of your hands out of water for long periods; wear gloves for dishes and cleaning.
  • Skip saunas, swimming, hard exercise and alcohol for about a week.
  • Put sunscreen on the backs of your hands too.
  • Switch heavy eye creams to lighter ones to reduce new milia.
